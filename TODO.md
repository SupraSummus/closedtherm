# TODO

- Only the setpoints are throttled; every reading (pressure, return temperature, modulation, fault) still costs an OpenTherm exchange on every pass. Give the slow readings their own interval the way `pushSetpoints()` has one.
- A setpoint write that fails is not retried until the next refresh, and `/` reports the requested value either way, so a rejected setpoint looks applied.
- Stale OpenTherm data: `/` reports the last values even when the boiler stopped answering; decide what to publish after N failed exchanges, then test it.
- `savePreference()` ignores what `Preferences` returns, so a write that saves nothing — a key over the 15 character cap, a full partition — passes silently and the setting reverts at the next reboot. Check the result and say so on serial, and in `/` if there is somewhere sensible to put it. (This cap is why the PI settings are named `pi_*`: the name is the key, so it has to fit in 15 characters.)
- The median in `thermometer.h` is aimed at supply dips under radio bursts, which is a hypothesis, not a measurement. Report the raw reading and `thermometer.median` on `/` next to the filtered value for a while to see what each stage actually rejects; `WiFi.setSleep(false)` is the one-line experiment that would steady the current draw if that is the cause.
- The median window is 16 `loop()` passes, so how long a dip it can reject is however long 16 passes take, and nobody has timed a pass. Time one, and if the loop ever slows down or speeds up much, give the window a length in seconds instead.
- Nothing checks the room sensor. A disconnected one reads near 0 mV, which `thermometer.h` turns into a few hundred degrees, and the PI controller answers that by asking for its minimum — the house goes cold with `/` reporting a plausible-looking setpoint. Reject readings outside a sane band and say so in the JSON; decide what the controller does then.
- `handleSet()` still names the sketch's own four settings three times over — validation branch, apply block, NVS key. `piSettings` in `pi_source.h` shows the shape that collapses it, but do not reach for it yet: those four are two bools and two floats, so it takes two tables and two lookups, and at two rows each the loops and declarations come to about twice the lines they replace. It starts paying once there are more of them, or once one type covers them all.
